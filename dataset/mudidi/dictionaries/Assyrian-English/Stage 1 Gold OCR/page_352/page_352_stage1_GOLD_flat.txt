<b>𒋗𒇻𒆪 𒀭𒀀 𒍢𒅔𒁲 𒅇 𒄵𒋼</b><!-- su-lu-ku a-na ẓi2-in-di u3 bir-te -->
<b>𒍪𒀪𒌑𒍪 𒌑𒉺𒄭𒅕</b><!-- zu-aḫ-u2-zu u2-pa-ḫi-ir -->, turi Babel-ki sa ana
resuti suluku ana ẓindi va birte zuhuzu upaḥir; <i>the young men of Babylon</i>
<i>who at the head went, to strong chains and bonds I brought.</i>—Black St. iv. 32.
These passages should explain each other, but any explanation I can suggest
is unsatisfactory; the chains and bonds may be simply guarantees and obligations,
because the conclusion which follows the last quoted passage states that the writer
aggregated these men to the people of Babylon, under restrictions.

¶ In the following passage I am inclined to compare <i>za'iri</i> with <b>צָעִיר</b>, "small,
weak:"—
<b>𒆷 𒈠𒄀𒊑 𒀀𒈾𒅈  𒀝  𒈪</b><!-- la ma-gi-ri a-na-ar ak-mi -->
<b>𒍝𒀪𒄿𒊑 𒈠𒁕 𒍑𒋼 𒅆</b><!--  za-ʾa-i-ri ma-da uš-te inu  -->
<b>𒉌𒌨𒍑𒋫 𒄠𒈪𒄴</b><!-- ni-ur-uš-ta am-mi-aḫ -->, la magiri anar akvi
ẓa'iri mada ustesir ma nisī ustammiḥ; <i>the rebellious I rejected, I burned;</i>
<i>the lowly greatly I have strengthened</i> [<i>or the poor I have enriched</i>], <i>and the</i>

I now think that <i>ẓa'iri</i> of the first extract is a variant spelling of <i>ẓayari</i>. See p. 338.

<b>𒆠𒄿𒉈𒅎 𒀭 𒍣𒀪𒉿 𒁲𒄭</b><!-- ki-i-bil-im an zi-ʾa-pi di-ḫi -->
<b>𒀊𒉌 𒈠 𒍏 𒆠𒆗𒋙 𒀾𒁍 𒊌</b><!-- ab-ni ma uruda ki-rib-šu2 aš2-bu-uq -->, kí ṭaim ili zihpi

See parallel passage in p. 224, under <i>Diddi</i>. In that passage I had forgotten
the value of <b>𒀭𒈠</b><!-- an-ma -->, "myself," and translated "by command of the god,"
instead of "by my own wish." Probably we have the same intention here, and
<b>𒈠</b><!-- ma --> may have been omitted by inadvertence. Read 79 for 71 in the note there. I
find <i>multa accepit</i> in Castell as a value of <i>za'afa</i> in the eighth conjugation.
