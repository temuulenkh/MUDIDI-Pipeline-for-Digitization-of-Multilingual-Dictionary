<b>𒀸 𒀀𒁲𒅀 𒊒𒂅 (𒌑) 𒆷 𒄑𒍮𒊒</b><!-- aš a-di-ia ah-dun3 (u2) la iz-zum-ru -->
<b>𒈠𒁁𒀭 𒎌𒃲𒎌</b><!-- ma-bat-an meš-gal-meš -->, sarri annuti mala apkidu ina adi-ya iḥtu
la iẓẓuru mabad ili rabi; <i>those kings, all that I had appointed, in alle-</i>
<i>giance to me failed, they did not maintain the service of the great gods.</i>—
Assur b..p i. 123.
<b>𒂃𒁉 𒈠𒆷 𒂊𒁍𒍑𒋗</b><!-- -->, gabbi mala ebus-su; <i>all</i>
<i>that which I have done (it).</i>—No. 13, E. 9.
Probably we should read here ebussu, an irregular spelling of ebusu, "(which) I
have done," the indirect form of the verb. The Persian copy has Tyamaiya kartam,
what by me is done.
<b>𒌑𒊌 𒃻𒆳 𒈠𒁕𒀀𒀀 𒈠𒆷 𒀸𒍑</b><!-- u2-uq gar-kur ma-da-a-a ma-la aš-uš -->
<b>𒆷𒅆 𒐊</b><!-- la-igi í --> ..., uqu sa Madai mala ina biti lapani-ya ... ; <i>the people</i>
<i>of Media, all who were in house, from me [revolted].</i>—Beh. 43.
Pers. Kára Máda hya <i>vithapatiya áha</i> hacháma hamitriya abava, "the Median
forces, which were at home (in their houses), against me revolted." The word
rendering "revolted" is destroyed in the Assyrian copy; the letters in Italic type
are barely visible in the Persian copy.
¶ <b>𒈠𒆷 𒁀𒋗𒌑 𒍑𒈠𒋗𒌑 </b><!-- ma-la ba-šu-u2 uš-ma-šu-u2 --> (v. <b>𒌋</b><!-- u -->),
<i>mala basú</i>, may be conveniently translated "all of them;" it is made up of the
preceding <i>mala</i> and of the verb <i>basú</i>, permansive form of <i>basa</i>, "to be." See
Rawlinson, in Journ. R.A.S. 1851, p. xcv., and Dr. Hincks, Journ. Sac. Lit. Jan. 1862,
and Grammar, Journ. R.A.S. 1866, p. 495 (16). The literal translation is "as many
as exist."
