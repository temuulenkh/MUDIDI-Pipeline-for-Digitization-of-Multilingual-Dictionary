UME 23 UTU
So; Nör; Nöt.—Full; Schüle, Scotch of School.
<b>U-MEN'-I-DEM</b>, <i>v.</i>, Ekpe, <i>n.</i> (Men. I.)
Money paid to egbo for recover-
ing a debt.
<b>U-MÖN'-Ö</b>, <i>n. Ib.</i>, <i>Ef.</i>, Ukurisü,
<i>q.v.</i>
<b>U'-NAM</b>, <i>n.</i> A disease resembling
epilepsy, to which children are
liable.
<b>U'-NAÑ</b>, <i>n.</i> (Nañ). Deceit. <i>Syn.</i>,
Abiaña.
<b>U-NÏM'-E-ÑK-PÖ</b>, <i>n.</i> (Nime, ñkpö).
One of supreme authority, whose
decision is final; a despot.
<b>U'-NI-ÖÑ</b>, <i>n.</i>, (Niöñ). 1. With ënyïn,
Fierceness of look.
2. Rapacity, greed. <i>Syn.</i>, Uñwai-
ënyïn.
<b>U-NÖM'</b>, <i>v.</i>, Unömö II., <i>n.</i> (Nömö
III.) Evil fate; fortune. Owo
unöm, An unlucky man.
<b>U-NU-AK'</b>, <i>n.</i> A crushing, a beat-
ing small; a kneading. Unuak
udia, <i>Food prepared so</i>, as fufu.
<b>U-NUÑ'-U-NUÑ</b>, <i>a. & adv.</i> (Inuñ).
Salt. Möñ emi etie unuñ-unuñ,
<i>This water is salt.</i>
<b>UÑ-WEM'-E</b>, <i>n.</i> (Nweme). 1. Greedi-
ness; covetousness; voracity.
Enye adia ñkpö ke uñweme, <i>v.</i>,
okpüm, <i>He eats voraciously, or</i>
<i>takes more than his share</i>. En-
yene, <i>v.</i>, anam uñweme, <i>He has</i>
<i>a greedy habit.</i>
2. With ikä. A putting one's
word into a matter belonging
to other parties; interfering.
Owo uñweme, ikä, <i>An officious</i>
<i>man. Syn.,</i> sometimes uñwo.
<b>UÑ-WOÑ'-KPÖ</b>, <i>n.</i> (Ñwöñ, ukpö).
A drinking vessel.
<b>U'-NYÏK</b>, <i>n.</i> (Nyïk). Compulsion.
<b>U-RUK'-N-KET</b>, <i>n.</i> (Ket). A plumb
line.
<b>U-RUK'-I-KO</b>, <i>n.</i> A plant of which
rope is made.
<b>U-SAK'-ËN-YÏN</b>, <i>n.</i> Wide awakeness.
<b>U'-SAÑ</b>, <i>n.</i> (Sañ). An oven or any
vessel or place where food is pre-
pared for baking. Usañ uyo,
<i>An oven for baking bread.</i>
<b>U-SAN'-A</b>, <i>n.</i> (Sana). Cleansing;
purification. Ñkpö usana-idem,
<i>Ib., a towel, &c.</i>
<b>U-SAÑ'-A-M-FI-ÄK-HÄ</b>, <i>n.</i> (Saña,
fiäkhä). A searching for something
lost or left; gleaning.
<b>U-SIR'-I-O'-BU-BE-A-BI-A</b>, <i>n.</i> (Siri).
The first thunder-shower after
the yams are dug.
<b>U-SI-NK'-HA</b>, <i>n.</i> (Siakha). A rent;
a breaking forth. Edem usiakha,
utïn, <i>The east.</i>
<b>U-TA'-OK-PO</b>, <i>n.</i> (Ta). A large
kind of ant which attacks man.
<b>U-TAB'-A-N-SÏN</b>, <i>n.</i> (Taba II., Sïu
J.) A small dish in which a child's
allowance of food is placed. <i>Syn.,</i>
Ñköp-nsïn.
<b>U-TAÑ'-A</b>, <i>n.</i> (Tañ I.) A picking up,
collecting. Ñkpö utaña ibök, <i>v.</i>,
mbiam, &c., <i>The offerings abia-</i>
<i>idiöñ prescribes.</i> <i>Syn.</i>, Ñkpataña.
<b>U-TI'</b>, <i>n.</i> (Ti). Remembrance;
memory.
<b>U-TI'-NK-PÖ</b>, <i>n.</i> A memorial.
<b>U-TÏÑ'-E-I-BAT</b>, <i>n.</i> A number which
cannot be equally divided; an
odd number.
<b>U-TÖN'-A-KAN-AÑ-WAN</b>, <i>n.</i> A fungus
growing on trees. <i>Lit.</i>, an old
woman's ear.
<b>U-TÖÑ-I-BU-O</b>, <i>n.</i> The side of the
nostril at its opening.
<b>U-TÜ'</b>, <i>n.</i> 1. Pertness. Enye otïm
utü ke inua, <i>He is pert, forward,</i>
<i>as a child speaking a secret which</i>
<i>he overhears.</i> Others, readiness of
speech.
<b>U'-TU-A</b>, <i>n.</i> (Tua). A weeping, cry-
ing. Eyën utua, <i>A whimpering,</i>
<i>fretful child.</i>
<b>U-TUAK'-HA-N-DU-Ä</b>, <i>n.</i> (Tuaka,
duä). Stumbling. Nkpö utuakha-
nduä, <i>A stumbling block.</i>
<b>U'-TU-AT</b>, <i>n.</i> An insect or reptile
resembling a frog, but much
smaller, which inhabits some
springs. Oñwöñ möñ nte utuat,
<i>He drinks like utuat</i>, as we say,
<i>Like a fish.</i> Èbüt aman utuat,
<i>Ib.,</i> e.g., <i>The goat is very pro-</i>
<i>lific.</i>
